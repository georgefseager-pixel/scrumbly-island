# Scrumbly Island

A light, playful 3D monster-catcher set on a tropical island. Explore, then find clever ways to lure and catch silly creatures called scrumblies.

## Play

Open `index.html` in a browser. You need an internet connection because Three.js and the fonts load from CDNs.

Playing with a controller (Xbox layout): the on-screen hints switch to controller buttons as soon as you touch the pad, and back to keys when you use the keyboard or mouse. Prompts, shop and menu footers, the title screen's controls, the hint bar, toasts and dialogue all follow suit (Ⓑ to talk and pick up, Ⓐ to jump, RT to use your item, LB / RB to swap, Ⓨ for the Scrumblydex, LT to run, ☰ to pause).

## So far

- A New Game tutorial with Professor Loveopolis: he gives you the net and asks for your first catch; is amazed (he's never caught one) and gives you the Scrumblydex; asks you to scan three kinds of scrumbly; gives you a cage trap and asks you to catch five; then shows you the Box and sends you off to explore and help the other islanders. An objective tracker and a "!" over the Professor show what to do next. Saves from before the tutorial skip it
- Pinksand Shore: a ring of pink-sand beach with shells, starfish, rocks, driftwood and palm trees
- Shallow water you can wade into, up to about 1 m deep
- A placeholder jungle in the middle, closed for now
- Palm trunks are solid and climbable
- A big catching net in first person: swing it to slam it into the sand (or splash the shallows)
- Gamepad support: left stick move, right stick look, A jump, RT/X use item, LB/RB or d-pad swap item, B pick up rock, Y Scrumblydex list, Back music
- The first scrumbly, the Bobo: a shy dumpling that pops a "!" and burrows into the sand when you get within about 6 m, then resurfaces once you're 12 m away or up a palm tree. Stun it by jumping off a palm onto its head or by hitting it with a thrown rock, then net it within 5 seconds. Or ambush it: leap off a palm next to one and swing your net straight away (within about half a second of landing) to catch it before it can dive New ones turn up elsewhere later
- Inventory hotbar along the bottom: the net, a stack of up to 10 rocks, the Scrumblydex and 3 cage traps
- A party of up to five scrumblies down the left; the first slot is your PARTNER. Each one you catch gets a random name for keeps, shown as "[Name] the [Species]" (from a list of ~500: normal, silly, cute, random words, and nods to Ouran High School Host Club, Ace Attorney, My Chemical Romance, Red Rising, Black Butler, Mistborn, Attack on Titan, The Summer Hikaru Died, The Witcher, Red Dead Redemption 2, Professor Layton, gothic literature, Danganronpa and The Lord of the Rings)
- Rare sizes: about 1 in 12 scrumblies is Big and 1 in 12 is Small. They look it, their hit and bounce zones scale to match, a scan points it out, and a catch is named "[Name] the Big Bobo" / "[Name] the Small Bobo"
- The Speshul, a little green monkey with big googly eyes: clings near the tops of palm trees and leaps to another tree as soon as you start climbing after it. Knock it down with a thrown rock (the aim arc turns pink when you're on target) and it tumbles to the sand, sees stars, then runs around in a panic, which is your chance to net it. After about 7 seconds it scampers back up a tree
- The Pengin, a very shy little green penguin: waddles about the sand near the rock nooks dotted around the beach. If it sees you within about 8 m it panics and runs flat out to its nearest nook and hides inside, only coming out once you've gone. Set a cage trap in front of a nook's doorway, then show yourself so it runs straight in
- The Duckshark, a shark that thinks it's a duck: floats, paddles, dabbles (tail up) and quacks out in the deeper shallows. Wade into the water near one and it charges and bites. Stun it with a rock from dry land, then wade in and net it while it's seeing stars
- Health: you have one heart (bottom left). A bite knocks you out and you wake up at the ranch, where Professor Loveopolis patches you up with one of several different pep talks. Visiting the ranch always restores you to full health
- The Weevil, a little brown sausage dog: races round the same looping circuit over and over, leaving a trail of paw prints in the sand. Far too quick to net, and a rock only makes it cross (it even speeds up). Follow the paw prints and set a cage trap right on its track
- The Beeappy, a big round happy bumblebee: drifts about overhead, bobbing between net height and well above the palm trees, and never lands, so traps can't catch it. Net it when it dips low, or leap at it from a palm. Hit it with a rock and it turns red and chases you to sting you, unless you net it first
- Scenery: an enormous Duckshark (45 times normal size) paddles in a slow circle far out to sea off the beach near the start, bobbing, swishing its tail, dabbling now and then and letting out a deep far-off quack
- The Beeangy: net a Beeappy while it's red and chasing you and you catch a Beeangy instead, a separate red, furrow-browed, permanently cross bee with its own Scrumblydex entry
- The Glimmery, the first forest scrumbly: a fluffy rainbow tie-dye fairy frog with shimmering wings and a star-tipped wand. Five flutter between the trees of Glimmerwood like Beeappies, but always just out of reach, leaving a twinkling glitter trail. A rock sends it zooming off high and away. An apple makes it dreamy: it floats down to just above the ground and drifts towards you for a while, so you can net it
- The Raebit: a soft tan bunny with long floppy pink-lined ears and pink foot pads. Four live in Glimmerwood. Each dances a little jig for a few seconds, then crouches and bounds clean across the forest in one enormous hop (around 40 m or more). Netting it mid-dance just makes it boing away; bonk it with a rock while it dances and it's dizzy for a few seconds, long enough to net
- The Blorbo: a squishy blue crocheted blob that lives in the forest river. Now and then a patch of river bubbles for a few seconds, then a Blorbo bursts up into the air, hangs there dripping for a moment and plops back in. It's too slippery to net, and a trap left where it surfaces gets knocked loose (back to your pocket), but a trap set while it's in the air catches it on the way down. Traps can now be placed anywhere you can walk, including the forest and the river bed
- The Willowet: a fluffy sage-grey bunny with big peach-lined ears that grazes out in the clearings, 9 to 15 m from any of the rabbit holes dotted round the forest (so there's time to get a trap in its path). Spot it and it bolts for the nearest hole, then tunnels off and pops up out of a different one far away, so you have to trap it on the way in
- Flops: a small pale bunny in a pink knitted top, scalloped blue crochet skirt and flowery ear linings. She hops about the forest floor and burrows away the moment she spots you (within about 11 m), like a Bobo, but she never comes back to that spot: she turns up somewhere else in the forest much later. Stun her with a rock from a distance, then net her while she's dizzy
- The Sita: a little red rosebud with a face, sitting perfectly still among patches of wild roses that look just like it. Bump into one and its thorns hurt you; net it from a safe distance. Worth very little gold, but keep one as your partner for 10 minutes (petting takes 10 seconds off, feeding it an apple 30) and it tries to get your attention: help it grow and it becomes a Rosita, a tall rose plant with a sweet tan face
- The Froggle: a huge, squat, warty lime-green toad with an olive back, heavy scowling eyelids and brows, orange cheeks and a big underbite with two little tusks, that charges and bites on sight. A rock bonks it back a step with a squash (it's far too tough to hurt, and it only gets crosser), snaps at nets and stomps traps flat. Its Scrumblydex tip hints at a soft spot for Bobos: with a Bobo as your partner it goes all soppy, follows your partner about with hearts, and can be caught
- The Pretzello: a little glazed pretzel with salt crystals and two black bead eyes, clinging to the foot of the fairy trees' trunks, glazed the same brown as its tree's bark so it blends right in (a shiny one wears a pink strawberry glaze). It never moves, and a net catches it easily, but don't dawdle: hang about within a few metres and your partner starts sniffing the air, then spots it ("Quick, net it!") and dashes over to gobble it up with a crunch, leaving you a message. A Pretzello partner won't eat its own kind
- Koneko (one of a kind): a big, friendly flying cat bus, a long fluffy tabby loaf with a brown back, three glowing windows down each side, six striped legs paddling at the air, a striped tail, big yellow eyes and an enormous grin. She flies a wide, lazy loop high over Glimmerwood, purring as she goes. Rocks just bounce off her fur and she snaps apples out of the air without slowing down, but her loop dips low and slow right past the treehouse's front balcony: stand there with your net and catch her as she drifts by (a hint pops up as she comes). Like Bink, she's unique, can't be sold, and stays out of the sky once she's yours. As a partner or house pet she floats along beside you
- Chupi, Mount Scrumble's mischievous little slingshot scamp: a small butter-yellow scrumbly with tall teal-rimmed ears, pink cheeks, a teal neckerchief and a fluffy teal-tipped tail, hopping about the plateaus with a wooden slingshot. When a Chupi can see you it takes aim, and a dotted red-orange line arcs from its slingshot to you (with a ring at your feet) before it fires a pebble that costs a heart. It can't see into the woods, and nothing gets through rock, so ducking behind a ledge or wall keeps you safe. Get within a few metres and it skips away; net it while it's lively and it ducks out. Bonk it with a rock to stun it (net it before it shakes it off), or corner it where it has nowhere left to run and net it outright. It happily eats apples, then reloads and keeps firing. A partner Chupi takes the odd potshot at a nearby wild scrumbly (teal aiming line), stunning it so you can net it
- The foothill ponds: three small, deep ponds round the foot of Mount Scrumble (too deep to wade), ringed with mossy stones, reeds and lily pads. Every so often one bubbles for a while. Toss an apple in while it's bubbling and out leaps a Friggly (any other time, the apple just sinks)
- Friggly: a big, soft, fluffy green frog with a long easy grin, the Mermiggly's cousin from up the mountain. Once out of its pond it races round and round a long loop along the foothills, leaping the two little streams that cross it. Rocks just bounce off it and it slips straight out of a net, so the only way to catch one is a cage trap laid on its loop (not on a stream: it's in mid-air there). After a couple of minutes it dives back into its pond
- Fraggly: very rarely, a Fraggly comes out of a bubbling pond instead. It looks like a Friggly but soot-black, with glowing red eyes and a scowl, and it hops straight for you and bites (a heart a bite, and a shove). Bonk it with a rock to stun it for a few seconds, then net it. If you get well away it gives up and dives back into its pond
- Titans: the giant scrumblies at the edges of the world (the Titan Duckshark out at sea, the Titan Beeappy high overhead, the Titan Mermiggly who rises to wave) can be scanned from afar with the Scrumblydex. Instead of a bio, a titan's card shows a `Titans Scanned 1 / 4` counter with a progress bar, and the count is saved. The total grows as more titans are added.
- Glimmer Pond: a big pond in a quiet glade of Glimmerwood (the trees grow round it). Its dark, deep water shades from pale shallows to an inky navy middle, with drifting glints, reeds, bulrushes and lily pads with pink flowers, and sits in a grassy berm with a mossy stone lip you can walk round. It's far too deep to wade into. Very rarely (every few minutes), an enormous Raebit, the Titan Raebit, slowly rises until his head and floppy ears poke out of the water, has a long look round, and sinks back down. Scan him while you can. Impatient? Throw apples into the pond: each one plops in with growing bubbles, and the fifth brings him up a couple of seconds later (throw one while he's up and he gulps it down)
- The carved pillar: a weathered stone pillar standing in the shallows, well round the beach from the other sea titans, crusted with barnacles and seaweed at the waterline. Its carved face looks out to sea, so from the beach it is just plain stone: wade round the back to find five engraved panels: Bobo, Weevil, Beeappy, Duckshark, Mermiggly. Examine it (E) and it buzzes with a strange energy, while any carving that already matches the scrumbly in that spot of your party (partner first, top to bottom) flickers faintly. Bring a party of exactly those five in that order and the carvings light up one by one with rising chimes, the sea rumbles ("something is coming!"), and an enormous Titan Bobo rises out of the ocean in a burst of splashes and ripples. It stays bobbing happily on the waves from then on (it's saved), ready to be scanned
- Bink, one of a kind: a small fluffy pink alien on a tiny flying saucer that putters round the whole beach in one long loop, never stopping or slowing. One rock just wobbles the saucer; after three hits it sinks to the sand and Bink is dizzy for a few seconds, long enough to net. There is only one Bink, so it is never Big, Small or Shiny, it is just called "Bink", and once caught it never appears in the wild again
- Cage traps: set one down and any scrumbly that walks over it is shut inside (Mermigglies stay in the water, so never). Collect it with E or your net before it rattles the cage and breaks out after about 24 seconds. Empty traps can be picked up and reused
- Professor Loveopolis's Ranch, just along the beach from the start: a thatched hut and a fenced pen with an open gate. The Professor, a big bearded frog-man in a lab coat, stands in the gateway. Talk to him to open the Box (48 slots) and swap scrumblies between it and your party. With a full party you can't catch any more: leave some with the Professor first. Up to five boxed scrumblies live in the pen (water-lovers in its pond) and crowd round with hearts and happy coos when you visit. You must always keep at least one scrumbly in your party, so your Partner slot is never empty
- Scrimple Scrumple, the first islander, appears once the tutorial is done: a lanky purple hippy frog-man in round orange shades and a tie-dye poncho, camped by an orange tent and campfire along the beach. He is horrified that you catch scrumblies (and that you are in league with his old rival Professor Loveopolis), so he buys them to set them free. His shop shows all your scrumblies with gold prices: trickier species are worth more, Big and Small ones 1.5x, Shinies 6x. Your partner and one-of-a-kind scrumblies like Bink can't be sold. Gold shows next to your health
- Scrimple's notice board unlocks after your first sale. It always has three requests for endangered scrumblies you've scanned: usually 3 to 5 of one species, sometimes one Big or Small one, and rarely (never more than one at a time) a Shiny. Pick a request you can fill, choose which scrumblies to release (the cheapest are picked for you), and get a much better reward than selling them one by one. A "!" over the board means you can fill a request. The next time you see Professor Loveopolis after meeting Scrimple, he has a grumble about that head-in-the-clouds hippy
- Glimmerwood, the forest: a tall flowering hedge rings the beach with four gaps, each under a flower arch and guarded by Sir Hopsalot, a brown rabbit knight in a helmet and breastplate (the same knight at every gap, somehow). He won't let anyone in, but 400 gold persuades him to step aside for good. Inside is a fairy forest: pastel-canopied trees strung with fairy lights, tree stumps, red spotted and glowing toadstools, mossy rocks, carpets of colourful flowers, fireflies, and a river winding right round the forest with eight lantern-lit wooden bridges. The island grew to make room: the beach is the same width, and the forest is now about 65 m deep (or wade across). A banner shows when you enter or leave. A bramble thicket rings the island's heart, with just one way through
- Mount Scrumble, the island's heart: through a single gap in the bramble thicket (by a `MOUNT SCRUMBLE: climbers only, please!` sign), sunny foothills at least 5 m wide ring a big tiered mountain of chunky, faceted pastel rock, with outcrops jutting from the cliffs, moss over the rims, and meadows of grass, flowers and pastel pines on every level. The smooth cliffs can't be climbed (bump into one and it tells you so), but the warm tan faces covered in knobbly handholds and vines can: walk into one, even at an angle, to climb it. One is straight ahead as you come in, with two more round the first plateau (17 m). The first plateau is cut in two by two ravines, crossed by jumping onto rock pillars (fall in and you're back in the foothills), and both faces up to the second plateau (26 m) are on the far side. Two faces lead to the third plateau (35 m), each with handholds starting 2 m up, reached by hopping up two boulders. From there a spiral of seven rock pillars, each a metre higher than the last, leads to the flat peak (42.5 m): pastel snow, a cairn, crystals, alpine flowers and a pink pennant you can see from below. You can hop on the boulders, your partner waits at the foot of a rock face while you climb it, then scrambles up the rocks to join you on each new ledge (and hops back down with you), and rocks you throw land on the ledges. The peak is where the next area will start from. Debug: Skip to Mountain starts you at the entrance with the beach and forest finished (every quest done, Lionel waiting up top) and one of every beach and forest scrumbly in your Box
- Lionel the Lilypad Leaper: a stocky red frog-mountaineer with big determined eyes, a yellow climbing helmet and head-lamp, a green neckerchief, a coil of rope slung across his chest, a blue harness, fingerless gloves, lug-soled boots, and a big orange backpack with a bedroll and an ice axe, standing in the gap to Mount Scrumble. He's conquered every peak in the land, his legs are killing him, and he isn't letting anyone else up first. Bring him a big partner and he scoffs; bring a full party of five Big scrumblies and he finally believes you're serious about big things... until the whole island shakes. It must be the Titans, so he wants you to scan at least five first, and while you're working on it he drops a hint about one you haven't scanned yet. With five Titans scanned he swears he saw a Titan give birth: there's a Giant Bobo on the beach. Catch it and he relents, hops off up the mountain, and tells you to find him near the top
- Giant scrumblies: a new size, far bigger than Big (2.5x), that never spawns by itself. Once Lionel is up on the third plateau, just below the peak, he has a hot scoop every ten minutes: he names a random scrumbly and where it lives, and a giant one of that species appears for you to catch. Sleeping or quitting sends it off ("The giant Willowet went back to its home planet. Probably."), and you can get a new scoop once his cooldown is up. Giants sell for 4x and wear a bold red-and-purple GIANT badge in your party and Box. Catch five (you don't have to show him) and he shouts down from the mountain and gives you the orange teleporter. After that he keeps handing out scoops, and he can also be found by the ranch
- The orange teleporter (slot 8): hold its button for three seconds to zap to the foot of your treehouse. Ten minutes to recharge, like the others
- Apples: 34 apple trees with white blossom grow in Glimmerwood, with apples hanging under their canopies. Climb to the top, look at an apple and press E to pick it, one at a time (up to 10 carried; they regrow after a couple of minutes). Throw them from slot 6 like rocks (they show the same aim arc). Hit a scrumbly and it gobbles the apple up with a munch and a burst of hearts; misses can be picked back up
- A giant Mermiggly lives far out to sea on the opposite side from the giant Duckshark. Every minute or so it rises up out of the water, waves over at the island with a deep, far-off ribbit, then sinks back down
- A giant Beeappy drifts in slow circles high above the island, with a deep, distant hum now and then
- Dr. Tealbottom, the second islander, appears after the tutorial: a teal frog-woman scientist in a lab coat, with goggles pushed up and a curly bun, by a satellite beacon that sweeps the sky and blinks. She's a colleague of Professor Loveopolis and runs three experiments. First, catch a Bobo without a rock (ambush it from a palm tree); she rewards you with a second cage trap. Second, prove the Beeangy exists by bringing one in your party; she works out it's just a Beeappy holding a grudge and gives you a health injection (a second heart). Third, prove the UFO is real by bringing Bink; she gives you the teleporter, a remote with a big red button that zaps you back to the ranch after a three-second hold (recharges in ten minutes, shown on its hotbar slot). Then she heads off to tell Loveopolis, and from then on stands beside the ranch with plenty to say. A "!" over her means she has something for you
- Lady Fluffytail, self-declared Princess of Glimmerwood, holds court on a pink velvet throne in a glade a little way in from one of the hedge gaps, between two potted rose bushes on a round pink rug. She's a tall white rabbit lady in a big pink ballgown with a white frill, pearls, puffed sleeves, a bow, a tiara with a heart gem, long-lashed eyes, an enormous powder-puff tail and a heart-shaped fan. You are her subject, and she makes demands. First, show her a shiny scrumbly (one as beautiful as her) in your party: she gives you a third cage trap. Second, three shinies in your party at once: she gives you a royal kiss (a third heart). Third, a giant rose: she's only ever found Sitas and wonders whether plant scrumblies grow with enough love; show her a Rosita and she gives you a green teleporter she found in the brambles, which zaps you to Scrimple Scrumple's shop after a three-second hold (slot 7, its own ten-minute recharge). Then she glides off to the ranch and stands beside Professor Loveopolis with plenty of royal opinions. A "!" over her means she has something for you
- Carlos the Capitalist, a grey rabbit in sunglasses, runs a makeshift stall a little way in from another hedge gap: a plank counter on crates under a red-and-white striped awning on branch poles, a hand-painted "CARLOS' DEALS: no refunds · no questions" sign, a heap of stones, a basket of apples and a scroll tied with a ribbon. He wears a loud orange flower shirt, white trousers, a gold chain and watch, has a slicked tuft, a toothpick and one floppy ear, bobs his head to music only he can hear and greets you with a wolf whistle and finger guns. He talks a big game, then opens his shop (in Scrimple's shop screen, in orange): throwing stones and apples for 20 gold each (up to your pocket limits of 10), and a one-off deed to a house for 600 gold. Buy the deed and he admits he has no idea which house it belongs to: he found it lying around in the brambles. No refunds
- Your treehouse: a big two-storey-high house up an old oak in Glimmerwood. Its site is picked before the forest grows, so it's always in the same spot, in a glade the trees keep clear of, with a clear sight line over the hedge to the beach. The oak has flared roots, branches and a huge leafy crown dotted with pink blossom. The deck sits 10 m up on the trunk, diagonal braces and four stilts. A 10 x 9 m plank house has open windows with white trim and flower boxes, a gable roof with a round attic window and a chimney, ceiling beams and two hanging lanterns. A balcony runs all the way round it, with a railing strung with fairy lights, potted plants at the corners, a bench and a brass telescope pointed at the beach. The ladder runs up the trunk to a trapdoor in the floor. Lord Bouncybun, Sir Hopsalot's cousin (a cream rabbit knight in gold armour with a purple cape and white plume), guards the ladder, waiting for the home's rightful owner to return. Show him Carlos' deed and he steps aside ("Funny. I always thought you'd be taller."). Climbing up is a little ride up the ladder; up top you can walk round the room and out onto the porch, and your partner waits at the foot of the tree. Carlos is somehow already inside: he dodges every question about how he got there, declares the place drab, drab, DRAB, and tells you to visit his stall, then vanishes. From then on his stall also sells one of each furnishing, delivered straight to the treehouse: Basic Furniture Kit (200: a four-poster bed with a heart headboard, pillows, a patchwork quilt and a bedside lamp; a round table with a cloth, teapot and vase; two cushioned chairs; a bookshelf of colourful books; a big rug and a potted plant), Poster Pack (200: scrumbly posters on every wall), Ranch Link (300: a green pipe that opens the Box), Easy Release Chute (300: a purple pipe that opens Scrimple's shop) and Bulletin Board (300: Scrimple's requests, by the door). Save up there and you'll load back in up there
- House pets: Carlos also sells pet licences, one at a time, each unlocking the next: Basic, Standard, Advanced, Expert and Master, 300 gold each, allowing 1 to 5 pets. The first one puts a woven pet basket with a pink cushion and a heart sign in the treehouse, plus a framed licence on the wall. Press E at the basket to pick which scrumblies from your Box live at home (pick again to send one back). House pets hop around the room (fliers hover), crowd round you with hearts when you're close, and no longer turn up at the ranch. Taking one out in your party sends it back to the ranch rules. Every time you climb into the treehouse, one of them welcomes you home ("Holiday is SO happy to see you come home!")
- Treehouse extras: nap in the bed (once you have the furniture kit) and the screen fades to a starry Zzz while you lie looking up at the beams; you wake healed and every wild scrumbly on the island (except Bink and the Blorbos) has wandered off to a fresh spot, including any that had run away. A zipline runs from a post on the front corner of the balcony, over the hedge, to a wooden tower with a flag on the beach. Hop on and you whizz down, speeding up as you go, and drop onto a big pink cushion (it only goes one way). Carlos also sells six wallpapers at 50 gold each: Green Grass (mint, with a meadow of grass and flowers round the bottom of the walls), Pink Petals (falling rose petals and little roses), Gothic Black (tiny skulls on a faint purple pattern), Sky Clouds, Candy Stripes (with little hearts) and Starry Night (gold stars and moons). He also sells six carpets at 50 gold each, laid wall to wall across the room: Cosy Cream (fluffy, with a pink scalloped edge), Picnic Gingham, Mossy Meadow (with little toadstools and clover), Rainbow Rings, Royal Purple (a gold lattice and border) and Ocean Waves (with little fish). Your first wallpaper or carpet brings a paint bucket into the treehouse (an open tin of glossy pink paint with drips down the side, a brush across the top and a few splats on the floor). Use it to switch between the wallpapers and carpets you own, or go back to bare planks and bare boards
- Lady Fluffytail's glade sits level on a pale stone plinth, so her podium no longer sinks into the slope
- Debug (title screen): Debug, then Skip to Forest starts you just inside a gap in the hedge with the tutorial done, the forest toll paid, 9999 gold, a Bobo partner plus one of every beach scrumbly in the Box (all scanned and caught), Dr. Tealbottom's quests finished (two traps, two hearts, the teleporter) and Scrimple's notice board unlocked. It replaces the autosave (click twice if there is one)
- Shiny variants: about 1 in 40 scrumblies has special colours (a golden Bobo, a purple Speshul, a pink Pengin, a blue Beeappy...). Wild Shinies sparkle, and a catch is named "[Name] the Shiny [Species]" (or "the Shiny Big Bobo"), with a rainbow Shiny tag in the party and Box
- Your Partner follows you around: it hops after you when you wander off, waits at the foot of trees you climb, floats in the shallows, and settles nearby to watch you. Look at it and press E to pet it for hearts, sparkles and a happy squint. Every few minutes it lets you know how it feels with a little note (about 70 different ones)
- Settings (from the pause menu): interface size, screen edge margin (for TVs that crop the edges), look sensitivity, invert look up/down, music and sound volume, and shadows on/off. Kept in this browser. A Controls page lists every key and controller button
- Performance: decoration and palms are culled in slices, scrumblies and the ranch are merged into few draw calls, far-off creatures aren't drawn, and the render resolution adapts to keep the frame rate smooth
- Saving: the game autosaves as you play (party, Box, Scrumblydex, where you're standing, rocks and traps, placed traps, music). The title screen offers Continue, New game and Load game. The pause menu has three save slots, plus Download save file and Load from a save file for backups or moving between computers. Saves live in this browser; the save file works anywhere
- The Mermiggly, half frog and half mermaid: splashes, spins and leaps about in the shallows and may stop to wave at you, but bolts and dives within about 9 m. Hit one with a rock and it gets cross and leaves for a while. Throw it an apple from the forest and it gobbles it up, does happy little hops, then swims over and follows you (waiting at the water's edge if you're on land); wade out and net it. A rock still sends even a friendly one off in a huff
- The Scrumblydex: a red handheld scanner. Point it at a scrumbly and click to scan it, which pops up its entry with a cute fact and a catching tip. Tab opens the full list of 60 entries (8 real, the rest placeholders): unseen ones are blacked-out silhouettes, scanned ones show their info, and caught ones get a badge
- Procedural calypso music and ocean sounds, made with Web Audio
